# BRI Domain Model

Status: Proposed conceptual model; not a database schema or approved domain vocabulary

## 1. Domain separation

BRI has a platform domain and customer information domains. A tenant/organisation is a security and configuration boundary, while customer-defined organisational nodes express business structure. Avoid assuming that platform tenant, legal entity, department, data owner and source system are synonymous.

### Platform foundation
- Tenant/organisation: customer environment and policy boundary.
- Principal: human, service account, application or agent identity.
- Membership: principal’s time-bounded relationship to a tenant or organisational node.
- Role assignment: actions a principal may perform in a defined scope.
- Policy: attribute/resource conditions that further constrain an action.
- Integration client: scoped identity for a connected system.

### Organisational model
- Organisational node: customer-configured unit/team/group/project/entity with type and metadata.
- Hierarchy edge: parent-child structural relation; cycle and reparenting semantics require an ADR.
- Organisational relationship: typed relationship that may cross branches or organisations and is distinct from hierarchy.
- Resource scope: tenant, node, collection or individual resource to which access rules apply.

### Information and knowledge
- Source system: external system or human/file origin.
- Source object/version: immutable source identity and version/hash where feasible.
- Extraction: parser-produced content/structure with tool/version and source locations.
- Interpretation: machine- or human-derived assertion, explicitly labelled and linked to evidence.
- Knowledge candidate: proposed structured content awaiting policy-required validation.
- Validated knowledge record: approved, versioned, scoped knowledge with provenance and lifecycle.
- Knowledge relationship: typed relation between validated records, with evidence and status.

### Decisions and intelligence
- Decision record: record of an actual organisational decision; not the same as a recommendation generated by BRI.
- Decision evidence: linked source/knowledge items and the role they played.
- Recorded reasoning: documented, partial, inferred or unknown; absence is not filled in by generation.
- Action/outcome: follow-up and observed result linked to decision and evidence.
- Intelligence run: scoped request and reproducible record of retrieval, calculations, model/configuration, response and evidence references.
- Insight/report: derived summary or metric with query scope, method, time and provenance.

## 2. Lifecycles and trust states

Source → registered version → extracted → interpreted/proposed → reviewed → validated/published → superseded/archived/deletion pending → deleted under policy.

These states are not interchangeable. An AI-suggested classification is not an approved classification; retrieved source text is not validated knowledge; a generated answer is not a historical decision; a decision outcome is not proof of causal effect.

## 3. Relationships

- Every tenant-owned domain object has a tenant association directly or through an integrity-enforced parent.
- Knowledge and decision evidence point to exact source/record version and location where available.
- Decisions may reference knowledge, source versions, actions and outcomes.
- Intelligence runs record which accessible versions were used, not just current record IDs.
- Cross-organisational relationships are typed and explicitly authorized; they do not implicitly grant access.
- Conversation/message records, if retained, remain separate from validated knowledge.

## 4. Design options requiring decision

1. Strict tree versus forest/DAG hierarchy; model hierarchy edges separately from cross-links. Recommended starting point: acyclic parent-child hierarchy plus typed relationship edges, supporting multiple memberships without multiple parents by default. Confirm whether customers require shared nodes/multiple parents.
2. Canonical universal ontology versus customer-defined types. Recommend stable core entity types and extensible customer metadata/taxonomies with validation; do not promise arbitrary schema mutation until governance and API compatibility are designed.
3. Decision modeling depth. MVP candidate: decision record, evidence, recorded reasoning status, date, owner reference, action/outcome links. Advanced alternatives, causal inference and workflow automation are later unless explicitly prioritized.
4. Temporal model. Preserve event/effective time separately from record/validation time; exact bitemporal semantics remain an ADR.

## 5. Domain invariants

- Access is evaluated against authenticated membership and resource scope, never labels supplied by a client.
- Provenance is mandatory for derived knowledge and important generated outputs.
- Superseded source versions remain identifiable for historical reproducibility subject to retention.
- “Unknown”, “not recorded”, “not reviewed” and “not applicable” are distinct states where their meanings differ.
- Human approval is recorded with actor/time/policy version when required before publication.
