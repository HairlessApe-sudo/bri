# Approved assignments: identity lifecycle and client administration

Approval of requirements is not acceptance of implementation. `main` at `2660e93` was clean at intake; application baseline `07958f6`. ## A10 — secure identity lifecycle and dual control

1. **Authentication and sessions:** Require provider-verified identity, verified TOTP/AAL2, active BRI membership and explicit permission at the resource. Server chooses tenant and role. Enforce a non-extendable eight-hour maximum BRI session; revoke on logout and invalidate after password reset, suspension or relevant grant/role changes. Reject stale/revoked sessions and cross-tenant access.
2. **Audit:** Append immutable security events for session start/revocation, authentication/MFA, recovery/invalidation, approval request/decision/expiry, reset execution/failure and privilege changes. Include appropriate actor, subject, tenant, action, time, outcome and correlation ID, never passwords, bearer tokens, reset links or TOTP material. Use existing audit infrastructure, transactional state/event changes and durable idempotent reconciliation for provider operations. Proposed configurable retention baseline: 365 days, pending legal/privacy review; no irreversible purge.
3. **Step-up:** Sensitive actions including reset requests and decisions require server-verified AAL2 and TOTP authentication within 15 minutes. Neither browser timestamps nor refreshed token issuance are evidence of recent MFA. Reauthentication must not extend the BRI session ceiling.
4. **Approval:** Reusable tenant/action/target/requester/policy/required-count/reason/expiry record, votes, execution state and immutable history, with deduplication and concurrency control. Initially only `USER_MFA_RESET`. Two different people, both active eligible same-tenant client Admins with explicit permission and fresh MFA: one requests, the other approves or rejects. No self-approval or single-admin exception. Request expires at 24 hours. Enforce `PENDING → APPROVED → EXECUTING → COMPLETED` and terminal rejection/expiry or recoverable execution failure. No duplicate external execution; quarantine/revoke old sessions and require controlled re-enrollment. Approval alone never means the factor was removed or gives account access.
5. **Client operation:** An active client requires at least two eligible administrators: active same-tenant client Admin with verified MFA, not suspended or pending deletion. Enforce at the persistence/transaction boundary including concurrent role, suspension and deletion changes. Keep a new client in provisioning until ready; initial bootstrap and exceptional recovery are platform-governed, never a client bypass.
6. **Provider:** Verify supported administrative factor operations. Server-only adapter; no browser credentials, secret disclosure or simulated production success. Persist recoverable provider failure/ambiguous outcomes and use idempotent reconciliation. If safe invalidation is not available, leave execution explicitly blocked.
7. **Acceptance:** Synthetic identities in two tenants; tests for authentication, expiry/revocation, reset, stale/missing MFA, separation, unauthorized/cross-tenant requests and approvals, expiry/rejection/replay/concurrency, audit integrity, provider partial failures/retries, session invalidation and minimum administrator/RLS enforcement. Full Python and web suites, typecheck, optimized web build, disposable PostgreSQL migrations, ledger checks and restricted-role integration tests. Record external provider blockers separately. Commit A10 independently before A11 implementation.

## A11 — client user administration (gated on accepted A10)

1. **Tenant-scoped administration:** Create, list/search, inspect, edit, suspend/reactivate and deactivate or request deletion of ordinary accounts; assign/revoke only permitted ordinary roles/grants. Use A10 to request/review MFA resets and view relevant administrative history. No peer-Admin or platform management, automatic promotion, MFA bypass, minimum-policy changes, secret access or cross-client enumeration.
2. **Provisioning:** Server-side provider adapter and exactly one active client for an ordinary active account. Secure setup with verified TOTP before activation. Model pending setup, active, suspended and deactivated; pending provider/BRI association gives no protected access. Handle duplicate/concurrent provisioning and retry partial failures safely. No plaintext credentials.
3. **UI:** Existing same-origin frontend conventions. Include searchable/filterable overview with status, safe MFA/grants and pending actions; detail, profile, history, creation and grants; reset request with reason/reauthentication; queue with requester, target, reason, expiry and explicit approve/reject. Distinguish requested, awaiting approval, approved, executing, completed, rejected, expired and failed. Never present approval as completed provider execution.
4. **API and database:** On every mutation verify authenticated principal, active membership, current tenant, role ceiling, explicit action and target scope, applicable fresh MFA and approval. Use a restricted runtime role and forced RLS. Preserve existing hosted-write restrictions. No client-selected authority.
5. **Deletion:** Deactivate safely; preserve foreign keys and immutable audit. No irreversible personal-data erasure before separate policy approval.
6. **Acceptance:** Test CRUD and denials for each role and across tenants; ordinary grant limits; immediate denial on an already-open session after suspension or relevant grant removal; provisioning/activation/duplicates/concurrency; MFA request/decision UI including expired and failed cases; provider failures, audit integrity, RLS, frontend integration, full regression suites. Verify locally; distinguish provider/deployment blockers. Separate A11 commit.

## A11.5 — Ordinary account deactivation

Approved on 2026-11-19 as a new subdivision of A11, not an original standalone assignment. An explicitly authorized same-tenant client Admin may deactivate an ordinary User or SuperUser other than themselves using fresh provider-verified TOTP, a reason and an expected version. Deactivation is BRI-only and distinct from both suspension and deletion. The transaction retires the target's active assignments, invalidates BRI sessions, and appends immutable audit while retaining identity, provenance and business records. No ordinary lifecycle route can reverse deactivation or restore grants. Any future restoration requires independently approved authorization and provisioning.

Keep forced RLS, tenant isolation, the two-eligible-admin invariant and default-deny hosted boundaries. Verify the isolated workflow in two synthetic tenants under the restricted PostgreSQL role, including denial, concurrency, rollback, assignment and session retirement, preservation of history and suspension, and migration checksums/replay. No provider deletion, irreversible erasure, restoration, deletion-request processing, deployment or A11.6 work is authorized.

## A11.6 — Read-only account administration history

Approved on 2026-11-19 as a new subdivision of A11. An active verified same-tenant client Admin needs explicit `account:history:read` and independent authorization to view an ordinary target, including a deactivated one. Reuse existing immutable provisioning, activation, display-name, suspension/reactivation, grant/revocation and deactivation events, without backfilling or fabricating records. Return only the event ID, timestamp, stable action, outcome, approved reason code where recorded and independently authorized actor ID/name; otherwise show a neutral restricted-actor label. No raw audit, provider identity, credential, MFA or recovery details, or free-form notes.

Provide a paginated GET and read-only panel. Preserve target not-found indistinguishability and forced RLS; verify under the restricted database role with two synthetic tenants, stable cursors, actor masking, audit immutability and no raw-audit access. No new account mutations, deployment, A11.7 or Data Disposition work.

## Boundaries for both assignments

Additive checksummed migrations only. No production authentication, hosted administration/configuration writes, public signup, real customer provisioning/data, shared migration, provider setting changes, deployment or push. Use synthetic fixtures and disposable PostgreSQL. Record test results and any externally blocked gate explicitly. No emergency bypass, no modification of prior migration files.
